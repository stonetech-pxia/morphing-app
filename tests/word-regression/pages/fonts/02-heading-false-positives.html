Chapter Seven

The ferry was late again, and the small crowd on the pier had begun to drift towards the café, where the owner was already stacking chairs against the rain. Agnes stayed by the railing with her suitcase.

“Why not tonight, then?”

Her brother did not answer at once. He watched the lights of the far shore come on one by one, as if he were counting them, and then he shrugged and picked up the heavier of the two bags.

The last boat of the season left the harbour at a quarter past nine.

Nobody on board spoke much during the crossing. The engine was loud, the benches were wet, and the captain kept the cabin door closed against the wind until they reached the other side.
