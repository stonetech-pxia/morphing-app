Chapter Three

The ferry left the harbour an hour late, and by the time it reached open water the wind had turned to the west. Marthe stood at the rail with her collar raised, watching the lighthouse shrink until it was no more than a white pin on the grey cliff.

Her brother had promised to meet her at the landing, but she knew him well enough to doubt it. He had never been on time for anything, not even for their father's funeral, when he arrived as the last hymn was being sung and stood alone at the back of the chapel.

When the island finally appeared, low and dark against the evening, she went below to fetch her suitcase. The cabin smelled of diesel and wet wool, and an old man was asleep on the bench with his cap pulled over his eyes.
