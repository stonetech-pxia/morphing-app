Seasonal Patterns in Small-River Discharge

Lea Marchetti · Jonas Feld · Ana Ribeiro

Abstract

We describe the seasonal behaviour of discharge in eleven small rivers over twenty years of daily records. A simple two-component model explains most of the variation: a slow annual cycle driven by snow melt and a fast response to individual rain events. The model is easy to fit and gives useful forecasts a few days ahead.

1 Introduction

Small rivers react quickly to rain, and their discharge is harder to forecast than that of large basins. Local authorities nevertheless need such forecasts to plan flood defences and water abstraction. Previous studies focused on large catchments where gauges are dense and records long.

In this paper we use the records of eleven gauges that have been operated by volunteers since 2006. The data are noisy but complete, and they cover catchments between twelve and ninety square kilometres. We show that a model with only four parameters per river reproduces the seasonal pattern and most of the short-term peaks.

2 Data

Each gauge reports the water level every fifteen minutes. Levels are converted to discharge with rating curves that are checked twice a year. We average the readings to daily values and remove days on which the gauge was frozen or under maintenance, which affects less than one percent of all days.

Rainfall comes from the nearest weather station, at most eight kilometres from the gauge. Snow depth is available for six of the eleven catchments; for the others we estimate it from temperature and precipitation with a standard degree-day method.
